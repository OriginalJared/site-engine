How to Choose a Smartwatch (Your Phone Decides More Than You Think)

By the Halcott Magazine editorial team · Updated November 2026

Before you compare a single feature, your phone has already eliminated most of the field. An Apple Watch doesn't work with Android. A full-featured Galaxy Watch really wants a Samsung phone. So the honest first question isn't "what's the best smartwatch" — it's "what's the best smartwatch for the phone I already own." Start there and the rest gets simple.

Start with your phone

This is non-negotiable and it saves you from buyer's remorse. The Apple Watch only pairs with an iPhone — full stop. On Android, you're choosing between Wear OS watches (Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel) and fitness-first brands like Garmin.

Samsung's watches work best with Samsung phones (some features need one), while a Pixel Watch or Garmin plays nicely with any Android device. Figure out your lane first, then compare within it. Cross-shopping an Apple Watch against a Garmin is mostly a waste of time if you own the wrong phone for one of them.

Battery life is the great divider

This is where watches split into two camps. Apple Watches and most Wear OS watches last about a day to two — you charge nightly, like your phone. Garmin and similar fitness watches last one to two weeks.

Neither is wrong; it's a lifestyle choice. If you want sleep tracking and hate charging, a long-battery watch is the move. If you want the slickest apps and don't mind a nightly charge, the mainstream smartwatches win. Decide which annoyance you can live with before you shop.

Health sensors: which ones you'll use

Every watch tracks heart rate and steps. Beyond that, the sensors worth caring about depend on you. ECG and blood-oxygen are nice for peace of mind but most people glance at them rarely. Sleep tracking is the feature people actually check daily — if that's you, prioritize a watch with good sleep analysis and the battery to survive overnight wear.

Don't pay a premium for a sensor you'll use once out of curiosity and then ignore. Match the sensors to how you'll really use the watch.

Smartwatch or fitness watch?

They overlap, but the emphasis differs. A smartwatch (Apple, Galaxy, Pixel) is a tiny wrist computer first — notifications, apps, payments, assistant — with solid fitness tracking bolted on. A fitness watch (Garmin especially) is a training tool first, with deep running/cycling metrics, GPS accuracy, and multi-week battery, and lighter smart features.

If fitness is the main reason you're buying, lean fitness watch. If you want an all-rounder that also handles messages and Apple/Google Pay, lean smartwatch.

Size, fit, and spending

Watches come in two case sizes for a reason — a big case on a small wrist looks and feels wrong, and a small case can be hard to read. If you can, check the sizes before buying. Also note that premium bands and LTE/cellular add cost you may not need.

On price: budget watches (Amazfit, base models) now offer GPS, heart rate, and great battery for around $200. The $350–450 mainstream flagships add refined software, better health sensors, and ecosystem features. Spend up for the integration and polish; save if you mostly want fitness basics and notifications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my phone determine which smartwatch I can buy?

Largely, yes. The Apple Watch only works with an iPhone. On Android you choose between Wear OS watches (Samsung, Google) and fitness brands like Garmin. Decide based on your phone first, then compare within that group.

How important is smartwatch battery life?

It's the biggest lifestyle divider. Apple and most Wear OS watches last about a day or two and charge nightly; Garmin-style fitness watches last one to two weeks. If you want sleep tracking and hate charging, prioritize long battery life.

Smartwatch or fitness watch — what's the difference?

A smartwatch (Apple, Galaxy, Pixel) is a wrist computer first with fitness bolted on. A fitness watch (Garmin) is a training tool first — deep sport metrics, GPS accuracy, multi-week battery — with lighter smart features. Choose based on whether fitness or everyday convenience is your priority.

How much should I spend on a smartwatch?

Budget options around $200 (like Amazfit) offer GPS, heart rate, and long battery. The $350–450 flagships add refined software, better sensors, and ecosystem features. Spend up for polish and integration; save if you mainly want fitness basics and notifications.
