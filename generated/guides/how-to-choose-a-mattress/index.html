How to Choose a Mattress (What Actually Matters)

By the Halcott Magazine editorial team · Updated November 2026

Buying a mattress online feels risky because you can't lie on it first — but that's exactly why the trial period, not the spec sheet, is the most important thing in this category. Beyond that, the right mattress is mostly about matching type and firmness to how you sleep. Ignore the buzzwords and focus on a few real factors. Here's how.

Your sleep position drives the choice

How you sleep matters more than any marketing claim. Side sleepers need enough give at the shoulders and hips to keep the spine aligned — too firm causes pressure-point pain. Back sleepers want balanced support that keeps the hips from sinking. Stomach sleepers need a firmer surface so the hips don't sag and strain the lower back.

Start by identifying your dominant position (and your partner's). It narrows firmness and type immediately. A mattress that's wrong for your position will cause aches no matter how premium it is.

The firmness myth

'Firmness' is subjective and not standardized — one brand's 'medium' is another's 'firm,' and the same mattress feels different to a 130-lb and a 230-lb person. Most sleepers land well with 'medium' to 'medium-firm,' but your body weight shifts how firm a bed actually feels: heavier bodies sink in more (so a bed feels softer), lighter bodies rest on top (so it feels firmer).

Don't fixate on the number. Use it as a rough guide, factor in your weight, and lean on the trial period to confirm the real feel rather than trusting the label.

Foam vs. hybrid vs. innerspring

All-foam mattresses (memory foam) contour closely, isolate motion superbly (great for couples), and cushion pressure points — but can sleep warm and give a 'hugged' feeling some dislike. Hybrids combine a coil support core with foam comfort layers: more support, bounce, edge strength, and airflow, which is why they're the popular all-rounder. Innersprings are bouncier and cooler but transfer more motion.

If you sleep hot or want responsive support, lean hybrid. If you love deep contouring and motion isolation, foam. There's no best type — only the best match for your feel preference and sleep temperature.

Temperature and couples factors

If you sleep hot, this matters a lot. Dense memory foam traps heat; look for gel infusions, open-cell foams, or (better) a hybrid with coils for airflow, plus a breathable cover. Cooling claims are often overstated, so weigh construction over marketing.

For couples, two things count: motion isolation (so you don't feel your partner move — foam excels here) and edge support (so you can use the full width and sit on the edge without rolling off — hybrids tend to win). If you share the bed, factor both in.

The trial period is your real safety net

Because you can't test it in a store, the sleep trial is the most important 'spec.' A good online mattress comes with a lengthy trial (often 100 nights or more) and free returns, so you can sleep on it for weeks and send it back if it's wrong. This is what makes buying a bed online sane.

Always check the trial length, whether returns are genuinely free, and the warranty. A mattress takes a few weeks to adjust to, so a trial shorter than a month isn't really enough. The return policy de-risks the whole purchase — don't skip reading it.

What to skip

Don't agonize over a brand's firmness number — it's subjective and weight-dependent; trust the trial instead. Be skeptical of dramatic 'cooling technology' claims; construction (coils, open-cell foam) matters more than marketing. Don't over-buy thickness or layers you don't need. And never buy a no-trial, no-return mattress online to save a little money — the trial is the entire point of buying a bed you can't lie on first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What firmness mattress should I get?

It depends on your sleep position and body weight, and firmness labels aren't standardized. Most people do well with medium to medium-firm, but side sleepers generally want more give for the shoulders and hips, stomach sleepers want firmer support, and heavier bodies experience any mattress as softer. Use the label as a rough guide and confirm with the trial period.

Is memory foam or a hybrid mattress better?

Neither universally — it's about feel and temperature. All-foam contours closely and isolates motion best but can sleep warm. Hybrids add coils for support, bounce, edge strength, and airflow, making them the popular cooler-sleeping all-rounder. Choose foam for deep contouring and motion isolation, hybrid if you sleep hot or want responsive support.

How important is the sleep trial when buying online?

It's the most important thing. Since you can't test the mattress in a store, a long trial (often 100+ nights) with free returns lets you sleep on it for weeks and send it back if it's wrong. A mattress takes weeks to adjust to, so always check the trial length and return policy before buying.

Do cooling mattress features really work?

Somewhat, but claims are often overstated. Construction matters more than marketing: dense memory foam traps heat, while gel infusions, open-cell foams, and especially hybrids with coils improve airflow. If you sleep hot, favor a hybrid or breathable foam with a breathable cover rather than trusting 'cooling technology' labels alone.
