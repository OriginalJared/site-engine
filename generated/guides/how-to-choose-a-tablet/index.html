How to Choose a Tablet (Be Honest About What You'll Do With It)

By the Halcott Magazine editorial team · Updated November 2026

Most tablet regret comes from buying for a fantasy. People picture themselves editing video and writing novels, buy a $1,300 tablet, and then use it to watch Netflix in bed. Be honest about what you'll actually do with it, and you'll either save a lot of money or buy the right expensive one on purpose. Here's how to think it through.

Start with the job, not the brand

Tablets split cleanly by use. For couch browsing, streaming, and reading, almost any tablet works and you should buy cheap — a Fire tablet or a budget Samsung does the job for $150–220. For real creative work (drawing, photo and video editing) or as a laptop replacement, you want serious power and you'll pay for it. For kids, buy something rugged and cheap with good parental controls, not a shrunk-down flagship.

Match the tablet to the task and the price sorts itself out. The expensive ones are only worth it if you'll use the power.

iPad, Android, or Windows

Three ecosystems, three personalities. iPads have the best tablet-optimized apps by a mile and the smoothest experience, which is why they dominate — if you have an iPhone, an iPad also just fits. Android tablets (Samsung, OnePlus) offer more hardware choice, expandable storage, and often more screen for the money, but fewer apps are built specifically for big screens. Windows tablets (Surface) run full desktop software and are really laptops that detach — great for productivity, heavier and pricier.

If you're unsure and you want the safe, app-rich choice, that's an iPad. If you want value or expandable storage, look Android. If you need real Windows programs, look Surface.

Don't skimp on storage

Base-storage tablets fill up faster than you'd think once you add apps, offline video, and photos — and many iPads can't take a memory card, so what you buy is what you've got. If you'll store movies for travel or do any creative work, step up from the base storage tier.

Android tablets often have a microSD slot, which quietly solves this problem for cheap. If you're torn between two Android tablets, expandable storage is a real tiebreaker.

Remember the accessories cost

This is the hidden line item. A tablet you plan to type or draw on needs a keyboard and/or stylus, and on premium tablets those can add $200–400 on top of the sticker price. A Magic Keyboard for an iPad Pro costs as much as a cheap laptop.

Budget for the whole kit before you fall in love with a tablet. Sometimes the honest answer is that a laptop would be cheaper and better for your actual workflow — worth asking yourself before you commit.

Screen, refresh rate, and spending

Screen quality is the thing you stare at all day, so it matters. OLED (on the iPad Pro) and AMOLED (on Samsung) are gorgeous for video; a smooth 120Hz display is lovely but not essential. For reading and browsing, a good LCD is perfectly fine.

On price: $150–350 covers streaming, browsing, students, and kids very well. $500–600 gets you a capable mid-range tablet that handles light creative work (iPad Air, OnePlus Pad 2). Above $1,000 is for professionals who need a genuine laptop-class machine. Buy at the tier that matches your real use, not your aspirational one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy an iPad or an Android tablet?

If you want the best tablet-optimized apps and the smoothest experience — especially alongside an iPhone — get an iPad. If you want more value, more screen for the money, or expandable storage, look at Android (Samsung, OnePlus). For full desktop software, consider a Windows Surface instead.

How much storage do I need in a tablet?

More than the base tier if you'll store offline video or do creative work — apps and media fill tablets fast, and many iPads can't add a memory card. Android tablets often have a microSD slot, which cheaply solves the problem.

Do I need to budget for tablet accessories?

Yes — a keyboard and stylus can add $200–400 on premium tablets, sometimes as much as a cheap laptop. Factor the full kit into your budget before buying, and ask whether a laptop would serve your workflow better and cheaper.

How much should I spend on a tablet?

$150–350 handles streaming, browsing, students, and kids well. $500–600 buys a capable mid-range tablet for light creative work. Above $1,000 is for professionals who need laptop-class power. Buy for what you'll actually do, not what you imagine you'll do.
