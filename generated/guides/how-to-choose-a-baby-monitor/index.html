How to Choose a Baby Monitor (What Actually Matters)

By the Halcott Magazine editorial team · Updated November 2026

Baby monitors split into two camps that barely resemble each other, and picking the wrong one for your situation is the main mistake parents make. Beyond that, the features that actually matter are the boring ones — reliable connection, battery that lasts the night, and audio that wakes you — not the long list of sensors on the box. Here's how to think about it.

Dedicated unit vs. WiFi camera: pick your camp first

A dedicated monitor comes with its own handheld parent screen and talks to the camera over its own private radio link — no internet, no app, no account. It just works, it's hard to hack, and it won't go down when your WiFi does. The downside: you can only watch from the included screen, within range.

A WiFi monitor streams to your phone from anywhere, adds smart features, and often has a sharper camera. The trade-offs are real: it depends on your internet, lives behind an account you must secure, and ties you to an app. Decide which model fits your life before comparing anything else.

Take the privacy question seriously

A camera pointed at your baby's crib is exactly the kind of device you don't want strangers reaching. WiFi monitors have been hacked, almost always because of weak passwords or no two-factor login. If you go WiFi, choose a brand with a real security track record, enable two-factor authentication, and use a strong, unique password.

If that whole category of worry isn't for you, a dedicated non-internet monitor sidesteps it entirely — there's nothing online to break into. For a lot of parents, that peace of mind is the deciding factor.

Range and connection reliability

For a dedicated monitor, range is everything — check that it comfortably covers your home, including going downstairs or out to the yard, and that it warns you when you're out of range rather than silently cutting out. Interference from other devices can shrink real-world range below the box number.

For WiFi monitors, the equivalent concern is a stable connection and low lag. A monitor that buffers or drops the feed when you most want to check is worse than useless. Reliability beats resolution here.

Battery and the 3 a.m. details

On a dedicated unit, the parent screen's battery life decides whether it survives the night on your nightstand — look for all-night standby and a clear low-battery warning. A unit that dies at 4 a.m. defeats the purpose.

The details that matter when you're half-asleep: audio that actually wakes you (plus a sound-activated LED bar so you can see noise with the screen muted), solid night vision, and two-way talk to soothe without going in. A big crisp screen is nice; dependable audio alerts are essential.

Sensors and 'smart' tracking

Newer monitors advertise breathing tracking, sleep analytics, cry detection, and room temperature or humidity. Temperature readouts are genuinely handy. Breathing and movement tracking can reassure some parents and stress out others with false alarms — and they are not medical devices, so don't treat them as one.

Buy these features if they'll calm you, but don't let them drive the decision or lull you into skipping normal safe-sleep practices. The core job of the monitor is to let you see and hear your baby reliably.

What to skip

Skip a cheap WiFi monitor from an unknown brand — this is the one category where a security shortcut isn't worth saving $30. Don't over-index on megapixels; night vision clarity and connection reliability matter more than daytime sharpness. And treat breathing or 'medical-grade' claims with healthy skepticism — they're consumer gadgets, not substitutes for safe-sleep guidance or a pediatrician.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get a WiFi baby monitor or a dedicated one?

Dedicated monitors use a private radio link with no internet or app — simple, reliable, and hard to hack, but you can only watch from the included screen in range. WiFi monitors stream to your phone from anywhere with more features, but depend on your internet and need a well-secured account. Pick based on whether remote viewing or simplicity matters more.

Are WiFi baby monitors safe from hacking?

They can be, but only if you take basic steps: choose a reputable brand, turn on two-factor authentication, and use a strong unique password. Most reported hacks trace back to weak or reused passwords. If that worry isn't for you, a dedicated non-internet monitor removes the risk entirely.

Do I need breathing or movement tracking?

Only if it will genuinely reassure you. These features aren't medical devices and can produce false alarms that stress some parents out. They're fine as extras, but shouldn't drive your choice or replace safe-sleep practices. A reliable view, clear night vision, and audio that wakes you matter more.

What range do I need from a baby monitor?

Enough to cover your whole home comfortably, including a floor below or a step outside, with margin to spare since walls and interference cut into the advertised figure. Just as important: the monitor should clearly warn you when you move out of range rather than quietly losing the feed.
