How to Choose an Air Fryer (What Actually Matters)

By the Halcott Magazine editorial team · Updated November 2026

An air fryer is really just a small convection oven with a fan cranked up, and that simplicity is why the category is full of near-identical machines dressed up with different button counts. The things that actually change your day-to-day experience are size, shape, and how easy it is to clean — not the number of preset programs. Here's what to weigh.

Basket vs. oven style

Basket air fryers (the pod-shaped kind) heat fast, crisp aggressively, and are the easiest to clean — you pull out one drawer. The trade-off is they cook in a deep, narrow space, so food cooks best in a single layer and capacity is limited by the basket.

Oven-style air fryers (a countertop box with racks) give you more usable surface area, let you see the food, and often double as a toaster oven. They take up more counter space and generally don't crisp quite as hard as a basket. If you cook for one or two and want max crunch, basket; if you want versatility and more volume, oven.

Capacity is measured in a confusing way

Air fryer sizes are quoted in quarts or liters of total basket volume, which doesn't map cleanly to how much food you can actually cook. A '5-quart' basket might only hold enough fries for two people in a proper single layer. As a rough guide: 3-4 qt suits one or two people, 5-6 qt covers a small family, and 8 qt and up is for bigger households or batch cooking.

The mistake people make is buying too small. If you're ever cooking for more than two, size up — crowding the basket is the number one reason air fryer food comes out soggy instead of crisp.

Dual-zone: genuinely useful or a gimmick?

Dual-basket air fryers let you cook two things at different temperatures and times, and finish them together — protein in one drawer, vegetables in the other. For anyone cooking full meals rather than snacks, this is the single most useful feature added to air fryers in years.

The catch is they're bigger, pricier, and each individual basket is smaller than a single large one. If you mostly reheat and cook one thing at a time, a single large basket is simpler and gives you more room per batch.

Cleanup is a daily reality, so check it

You'll clean this thing constantly, so the basket and tray should be nonstick and dishwasher-safe. Look for a design where the basket detaches fully from any crumb tray — fixed-grate designs trap grease underneath where it's a pain to reach.

Also consider the drawer mechanism: a basket that slides out cleanly beats one you have to wrestle. This is the kind of detail that decides whether the air fryer stays on the counter or gets exiled to a cabinet after a month.

Temperature range and presets

A good air fryer should reach around 400°F — that top-end heat is what produces real crisp. Most do. The preset buttons (fries, chicken, fish) are mostly marketing; they just set a temperature and time you could dial in yourself, and you'll quickly ignore them.

What's genuinely nice is a simple, readable display and a shake reminder. A window and interior light are pleasant on oven-style models. Don't pay a premium for a dozen presets you'll never trust over your own eyes.

What to skip

Skip app connectivity — nobody needs to control an air fryer from their phone, and it's a feature that adds cost and failure points. Be skeptical of huge preset counts; they're a button-count arms race, not a cooking upgrade. And don't buy the smallest model to save counter space if you'll regret the capacity — a too-small air fryer gets used once a week instead of every day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size air fryer should I buy?

Match it to your household: 3-4 quarts for one or two people, 5-6 quarts for a small family, and 8 quarts or more for larger families or batch cooking. Most people regret buying too small, because overcrowding the basket makes food soggy instead of crispy.

Are dual-zone air fryers worth it?

If you cook full meals, yes — being able to run a protein and a side at different temperatures and finish them together is genuinely useful. If you mostly reheat or cook one item at a time, a single large basket is simpler and gives you more room per batch.

Basket or oven-style air fryer — which is better?

Basket models heat fast, crisp hard, and clean easily but cook in a smaller single-layer space. Oven-style models offer more capacity, let you see the food, and often double as a toaster oven, but take more counter space and crisp slightly less aggressively.

Do air fryer preset buttons actually matter?

Not much. Presets just apply a preset temperature and time you could set yourself, and most people stop trusting them once they learn their machine. Prioritize a strong top temperature (around 400°F), easy cleanup, and the right capacity over the number of preset programs.
